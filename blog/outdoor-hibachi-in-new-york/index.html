New York

Outdoor hibachi in New York

Terraces and balconies more often than a lawn. The grill still stays outside.

Outdoor hibachi in New York is usually a terrace, a balcony, or a backyard behind a house, not a restaurant dining room. The chef arrives with the grill and the ingredients, sets up outside, and cooks there. Guests can eat outdoors with the show or at a table inside. Indoor dining is fine. Indoor cooking is not.

Tight outdoor space

Say so if the terrace is small, the awning is low, or the path to the grill is a narrow stair. Those details decide whether the spot is safe. They do not change the plate. A patio, a terrace, and a true backyard are all on the New York list when the chef can stand outside with the grill.

What the evening is

This is a private party at the home. You keep the guest list. The chef does the teppanyaki show and the dinner: salad, vegetables, fried rice, and two proteins. Chicken, steak, shrimp, salmon, and tofu are included at $50 per person. Children 12 and under are $25. The minimum is $500 even for a smaller terrace dinner.

Upgrades and the house rules

Scallops and filet mignon are +$5. Lobster tail is +$15. Noodles are +$4. You provide tables, chairs, plates, and utensils, which matters in a New York apartment where the rental may not have a full set for every guest. No nuts or sesame. Gluten-free sauces if you ask. Tofu for vegetarian guests at the same price. Gratuity is extra, suggested at 20%. Cash on the day, no deposit.

The New York calendar

New York has its own booking calendar. Reserve there, then watch for the confirmation email. Support contacts you about six days before. If the headcount changes, call or text (929) 992-9932. Parties of 30 or more adults are booked with two chefs, which is a tight fit on a small terrace, so mention the outdoor space when the list is that large.
